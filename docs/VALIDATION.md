# Retrospective literature reconstruction & external benchmarking

Everything on this page is regenerated by

```bash
python reproduce.py
```

which writes the audited long form to [`results/`](../results). This file summarises
[`results/validation.txt`](../results/validation.txt); where the two ever disagree, the generated
file is authoritative, because it is produced by running the engine.

> The shipped defaults are literature-typical starting points. These comparisons show that the
> *methodology* is sound and the numbers are *plausible* — not that any single scenario matches a
> specific plant. Replace the defaults with measured data before drawing conclusions.

## None of this is prospective validation

Every source below was already in print when its scenario was built, and no prediction was
registered before the target was read. The dataset used to call its strongest cases **blind**;
they were not, and the word has been removed. Comparisons are classified instead by **what each
one depends on** — recorded per record in
[`data/studies/studies.yaml`](../data/studies/studies.yaml) as `evidence_class`, with the
vocabulary in [`schema.py`](../src/algametrix/paper/schema.py).

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **retrospective untuned** | The source's own assumptions were entered without adjusting anything to its reported endpoint. Untuned, but not blind: the endpoint was available, and no pre-specification is claimed. |
| **component-informed** | A published *aggregate outcome* of the source was used to build one or more model inputs, so part of the target appears on both sides of the comparison. Not an independent endpoint test. |
| **calibrated** | The scenario was deliberately configured to reproduce a reference simulator or case study, to show the engine agrees with it under matched assumptions. |
| **range** | A plausibility check against a published envelope. Never turned into a percentage deviation from an envelope midpoint. |
| **excluded** | The reported figures could not be traced to the cited source. The record stays in the dataset with its reason, and enters no population. |

**The classes are never pooled**, and only the untuned rows carry a reported range.

Two further dependencies are fields rather than prose. `author_overlap_with_algametrix` says
whether an author of AlgaMetrix is an author of the source. `independence_group` names the
publication, facility model and author group, so that several scenarios of one paper are counted
as several comparisons and **one** independent source.

### Deviations below 2 % carry a decimal

Rounding +1.4849 % to +1 % moves the number by a third of its own value. Point deviations under
2 % are therefore printed to one decimal; everything above that is whole percent, which is the
resolution a reconstruction built from published tables can carry. An interval already states its
own spread and both of its ends are given in whole percent.

### Biogenic carbon is not assumed either

A percentage between two different biogenic-carbon conventions is no more a deviation than a
percentage between two currencies. Every GWP comparison is therefore placed on the strongest
available basis: on the source's **gross** figure where it publishes one, because a gross result
carries no convention; otherwise on the net, where the source's convention is recorded. Where the
convention is unrecorded, the row is **refused** unless the engine's own biogenic adjustment is
below 1 % of its gross, in which case no convention could materially move it.

### Currency is not assumed

**AlgaMetrix has no currency of its own**: it returns costs in the units of the price set it is fed.
Every cost comparison therefore records the basis of the engine value, the basis of the source
value, and the transformation between them — see [PRICE_BASIS.md](PRICE_BASIS.md).

A currency change whose denomination year is unknown is **refused, not approximated**: a flat
exchange rate would fold a currency movement into the deviation. Where a scenario mixes source
prices with shipped defaults, it is re-run with those defaults expressed in the source's currency
and the deviation is reported as the interval the two readings span.

## 1. Production cost — retrospective untuned reconstructions

| Study | System | Model | Source | Basis | Deviation |
|---|---|---|---|---|:---:|
| Tredici et al. 2016 | flat-panel PBR | 12.29 | 12.11 *(12.40 raw)* | EUR 2016 | **+1.5%** |
| Vázquez-Romero et al. 2022 (STOTEN), *N. oceanica*, 10 ha | stacked tubular PBR | 33.59 | 36.21 | EUR 2021 | −7% |
| Vázquez-Romero et al. 2022 (STOTEN), *P. tricornutum* / *T. lutea* | stacked tubular PBR | 93.18 | 105.2 | EUR 2021 | −11% |
| Vázquez-Romero et al. 2022 (STOTEN), *N. oceanica* | stacked tubular PBR | 46.09 | 53.32 | EUR 2021 | −14% |

**Range: −14% to +1.5%**, over **4 scenarios from 2 publications**. Functional unit: 1 kg dry
biomass.

The three STOTEN rows are one publication, one facility model and one author group
(`independence_group: vazquez_stoten_2022`) — the best-documented source in the set, whose
supplementary tables give a complete price set — and they carry the three largest negative
deviations. The gap sits in operating-cost lines a mass balance cannot derive: consumables,
wastewater treatment, cooling water, contingencies.

### The Tredici endpoint is harmonized

The published EUR 12.40/kg is not the endpoint AlgaMetrix computes. The source's plant carries
EUR 446,367/yr of total annual cost over 36,000 kg/yr — which is that 12.40 — and EUR 10,236/yr
of it is **financing interest**. The AlgaMetrix production cost carries no financing charge and
no required return on capital, so the two include different things. Removing the interest term
puts the source on the engine's endpoint:

```
(446367 − 10236) / 36000 = 12.114750 EUR2016/kg
```

Against that, the engine's 12.294638 is **+1.5%** (+1.4849% unrounded). Both readings stay in the
record — `source_value: 12.40`, `harmonized_value: 12.114750` — and the arithmetic is written out
in `endpoint_harmonization`, so the harmonization can be undone by anyone who disagrees with it.

## 2. Production cost — component-informed

| Study | System | Model | Source | Basis | Deviation |
|---|---|---|---|---|:---:|
| Vázquez-Romero et al. 2022 (Algal Res.) | tubular PBR + LED | 104.3 | 108.3 | EUR 2021 | −4% |

Reported on its own, and **never pooled with the untuned rows**, because two of its inputs are
built out of the paper's own published total OPEX of EUR 2,228,066/yr: labour is 0.3879 × that
total, and the other-OPEX line (thermal utilities, consumables, wastewater) is 0.0949 × it.
0.3879 + 0.0949 = 0.4828, so **48.28 % of the numerator being compared comes from the target
itself**. The −4% is partly a self-comparison.

## 3. Calibrated benchmarks

A calibrated case is configured to reproduce its reference. Agreement therefore tests the
implementation against a reference tool under matched assumptions; **it is not independent
validation and is not evidence of predictive accuracy.**

| Case | Script | Model | Source | Basis | Deviation |
|---|---|---|---|---|:---:|
| Algal oil in raceway ponds (Carmichael & Petrides 2020) | `phototrophic_algal_oil.py` | 1.673–1.711 | 1.70 | USD 2015 | −2% to +1% |
| Omega-3 fermentation (Flevaris et al. 2021) | `omega3_fermentation.py` | 16.35–17.55 | 16.57 | USD 2021 | −1% to +6% |
| Heterotrophic fermenter (Russo et al. 2022) | `heterotrophic_powder` | 17.31–17.47 | 16.46 | USD 2022 | +5% to +6% |
| Spirulina raceway GWP (Ahmed et al. 2026) | `spirulina_padi` | 14.24 | 15.05 | kg CO₂-eq/kg | −5% |

The two SuperPro cases are mixed price sets, hence the intervals.

Russo et al. 2022 is both a **self-citation** (`author_overlap_with_algametrix: true`) and a
calibration: the scenario is the shipped heterotrophic template, whose parameters were set
against the SuperPro reference case published with the paper. Ahmed et al. is calibrated because
its scenario builder reconstructs the Padi et al. (2023) plant the study's design follows, not
this source's own data.

Both are Intelligen's own published SuperPro Designer studies and both are retrievable without a
licence: [10.13140/RG.2.2.12639.48805](https://doi.org/10.13140/RG.2.2.12639.48805) for the algal
oil case and [10.13140/RG.2.2.32102.34885](https://doi.org/10.13140/RG.2.2.32102.34885) for the
omega-3 case. A reader can therefore check what the engine was calibrated against. Until these
rows carried a DOI they named a report rather than a document, and a calibration target nobody
else can open is not evidence.

The algal-oil study is dated 2020 while the economic evaluation it exports is in 2015 prices. The
basis column follows the prices, not the publication date.

## 5. Cradle-to-gate GWP — retrospective untuned

| Study | System | Basis compared | Model | Source | Deviation |
|---|---|---|---|---|:---:|
| McKuin et al. 2022 | *Schizochytrium*, fermenter | net; engine adjustment 0.000, convention-free in practice | 2.385 | 2.33 | +2% |
| Tzachor et al. 2022 | Spirulina, geothermal LED PBR | **gross**; conventions verified and matched | 1.310 | 1.262 | +4% |
| Braud et al. 2025 (SpiralG) | Spirulina raceway, NaHCO₃ | net; conventions matched | 12.61 | 14.10 | −11% |

**Range: −11% to +4%**, over 3 scenarios from 3 publications. Functional unit: 1 kg dry biomass,
kg CO₂-eq/kg. The Ahmed et al. 2026 GWP case is calibrated and is reported in §3, not here.

Three things a reader needs in order to use these numbers:

1. **The conventions have been checked against the sources, and the comparisons moved onto bases
   that do not depend on them.** Tzachor et al. apply an explicit −0.702 kg CO₂-eq/kg credit for
   CO₂ biofixation (AlgaMetrix's `source_specific_credit`) and report a net of −0.008 per kg *wet*
   biomass at 61 % water, including construction. Placed on the engine's basis — per kg dry,
   operational — that is a gross of 1.262, a credit of −1.800 and a net of −0.539, and the
   comparison is made on the **gross**, which carries no convention. Every figure on the source
   side is *derived* from the paper's own components by one declared division, not printed by it,
   which makes this row weaker than a comparison against a published value. The published
   correction to that paper (electricity per FU 137.9 → 54.48 kWh) was checked; the reconstruction
   already uses the corrected figure. Ahmed et al. apply no credit at all, and the engine applies
   none to a bicarbonate-fed scenario, so both sides are gross.
2. **SpiralG is not like-for-like.** The engine reproduces the operational path only; the published
   total also includes infrastructure and transport (~2 kg CO₂-eq/kg), which accounts for most of
   the −11%.
3. Four biogenic-carbon conventions are implemented and gross and net are always reported together,
   so the choice can be undone — see [CARBON_ACCOUNTING.md](CARBON_ACCOUNTING.md).

## 6. Internal verification

Three checks, all run over the same 27-scenario suite (`algametrix.paper.suite`), so they
describe one population. **None of them is an accuracy claim** — they are statements about the
implementation, and all three sit at floating-point resolution because they are supposed to.

| Check | File | Scale | Max relative residual |
|---|---|---|---|
| Construction identities (163) and physical-admissibility constraints (147) | [`results/verification.txt`](../results/verification.txt) | 27 scenarios | 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁶ |
| **Shared-inventory consistency**: the quantity the TEA priced vs the quantity the LCA characterized, each recovered independently from the published results | [`results/shared_inventory_consistency.txt`](../results/shared_inventory_consistency.txt) | 167 flow comparisons | 1.2 × 10⁻¹³ |
| **LCA implementation**: sequential engine vs an independent matrix-formalism implementation (`A s = f`) | [`results/lca_implementation_benchmark.txt`](../results/lca_implementation_benchmark.txt) | 243 indicator comparisons | 2.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ |

Optionally, the same matrices solved by Brightway's `bw2calc` agree to 4.9 × 10⁻¹⁵ over 189
comparisons, 180 of them non-zero on both sides
([`results/brightway_crosscheck.txt`](../results/brightway_crosscheck.txt)); that check runs
from `scripts/brightway_crosscheck.py` in its own environment, because `bw2calc` pulls in around
thirty packages the engine does not need.

The consistency result is an **architectural invariant**: `run_scenario` builds one `Inventory`
and hands the same object to both analyses, so agreement is guaranteed by the call graph rather
than discovered. It is measured anyway, because sharing an object does not guarantee that both
analyses read the same *field* of it, and because recovering the quantities from the *results*
also covers everything between the inventory and the reported number — overheads, allocation,
functional unit.

The same file carries a controlled counter-example on two scenarios: emulating duplicated
inventories and applying one physical change (harvesting recovery 0.90 → 0.765) to the TEA copy
only opens a 15 % divergence in the flows and understates the reported GWP by **8.5 %** in the
heterotrophic case and **13.7 %** in the Spirulina raceway, while the reported cost is unchanged
in both. That is a demonstration of a failure mode run on this engine against itself, not a claim
about any other software.

## 8. What the evidence base is

| | |
|---|---|
| Point comparisons | **12** |
| — retrospective untuned | **7** (4 production cost from 2 publications, −14% to +1.5%; 3 GWP from 3 publications, −11% to +4%) |
| — component-informed | **1** (−4%, 48.28 % of its numerator taken from the target) |
| — calibrated | **4** (2 vendor SuperPro cases, 1 self-citation, 1 GWP) |
| Range plausibility checks | 1 (**outside** its envelope) |
| Excluded for untraceable provenance (2026-08-07) | 2 |
| Excluded earlier, no primary source (`scp_protein`) | 1 |
| Distinct publications behind the 12 | 11 (9 peer-reviewed + 2 vendor simulator reports) |

It supports the plausibility of the implementation across open ponds, photobioreactors and
heterotrophic fermenters, for whole biomass and extracted products. It does **not** establish
predictive accuracy for untested configurations, and nothing here is prospective validation:
every target was already published when its scenario was built. The base is small and partly
clustered — three of the four untuned cost scenarios are one publication, and two of the four
calibrated cases are driven by the same scenario builder. The only surviving range check is one
the engine fails, and the base became *smaller* at the last revision rather than larger.

A stronger programme would add prospective, independently implemented holdout cases whose
predictions are fixed before the reference result is inspected — especially for phototrophic LCA
and multi-product biorefineries. None exists yet, and this page does not pretend otherwise.
