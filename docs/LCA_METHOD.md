# The life-cycle method: what AlgaMetrix computes, and what it does not

An impact number is not interpretable without the method behind it. This
document states what the environmental calculation is — its goal and scope,
boundary, cut-off, allocation, impact-assessment method and background data —
and, in the same detail, what it leaves out.

The authoritative source is **[`data/lcia.yaml`](../data/lcia.yaml)**: the same
file the engine loads its factors from carries the declaration around them, so
the two cannot drift apart. Print the whole thing with

```bash
python -m algametrix.lciamethod
```

Generated evidence: `results/lcia_method_statement.txt`, regenerated by
`python reproduce.py --only method`, which adds a completeness table over the
scenario suite.

## 1. Goal and scope

| | |
|---|---|
| Study type | attributional, process-based, linear, steady-state, average (not marginal) |
| Boundary | cradle-to-gate |
| Functional unit | 1 kg of dry biomass at the plant gate, or 1 kg of the designated main product in a multi-product configuration |
| Reference flow | the gross biomass, energy, carbon, nutrient, water and land flows needed to deliver that kilogram |
| Geography | EU-27 average; **not regionalized** |
| Reference period | roughly 2019–2022 |
| Background database | none — see §4 |

The calculation is organised in the four phases of ISO 14040 (goal and scope,
inventory, impact assessment, interpretation), and `data/lcia.yaml` is the scope
declaration ISO 14044 clause 4.2.3 asks a study to make. Conformance itself is a
property of a study, not of a program. A study made with AlgaMetrix conforms only
if its author states goal and scope, replaces the shipped defaults with data
representative of the system studied, and — for comparative assertions disclosed
to the public — obtains the critical review ISO 14044 clause 6 requires. The
defaults do not meet that condition on their own.

## 2. Inside the boundary

Cultivation (carbon supply as CO₂ or bicarbonate, nitrogen, phosphorus, process
water, site land occupation, cultivation and circulation electricity),
harvesting and dewatering electricity, drying heat and electricity, downstream
disruption and extraction electricity, solvent-recovery heat and net solvent
make-up, declared materials and utilities, the direct nitrogen and phosphorus
release to water, and the biogenic carbon fixed into the biomass under the
declared convention.

## 3. Outside it

| Excluded | Note |
|---|---|
| Infrastructure and capital goods | Ponds, reactors, fermenters, buildings, piping, membranes and their replacement are costed in the TEA and absent from the LCA. Published algal LCAs put infrastructure at a few per cent of GWP for open ponds and materially more for closed reactors and thin films, so the exclusion is **not neutral for a comparison between reactor types**. |
| Transport | Inbound nutrients, substrate, solvent, CO₂; outbound product. |
| Packaging | Primary and secondary. |
| End-of-life and use phase | Outside a cradle-to-gate boundary by definition. |
| Spent-medium treatment | Only the direct nutrient release is modelled, through the partitioning fractions in §5. |
| Cleaning and sterilisation chemicals | Declare them as materials where they matter. |
| Direct emissions other than N and P to water | No CH₄, N₂O, NH₃ or VOC release from the culture, the drying stack or solvent losses. |
| Labour and overhead | Cost in the TEA, no burden in the LCA. |

**The cut-off rule is structural, not a threshold.** A percentage cut-off
presupposes a complete inventory from which small contributors are then dropped.
Here the boundary is set by what the process model represents: everything in the
table above is absent by construction, not because it fell below 1% of mass,
energy or cost. A study that needs a conventional cut-off statement has to
establish it against a complete database-backed inventory, which this tool does
not build.

## 4. Background data

AlgaMetrix ships **no LCI database** and needs none to run. The background is a
table of aggregated cradle-to-gate factors: one already-characterized number per
input per category (kg CO₂-eq per kWh, not kg CH₄ per kWh). Each carries its
unit, geography, reference period, source and a quality flag —
`sourced`, `derived` or `indicative`.

Four consequences follow, and they are declared rather than implied:

1. **No elementary flows.** Contribution analysis stops at the input; no
   substance-level result exists.
2. **The characterization step is nearly empty.** In the matrix formulation of
   the same calculation (`algametrix.paper.matrixlca`) the characterization
   matrix **C** is close to an identity mapping. An independent matrix or
   Brightway run over these factors therefore verifies the linear algebra and
   the foreground — not the characterization.
3. **No regionalization and no database uncertainty.** The factors are point
   values with no location dependence.
4. **Most defaults are indicative.** 22 of the 23 shipped factors are
   literature-typical order-of-magnitude values, chosen so the software runs out
   of the box. The acidification and eutrophication factors in particular carry
   no traceable dataset. Replace them with data from a declared database before
   publishing a plant-specific result, and record the database name and version
   in `data/lcia.yaml`.

## 5. Impact categories

| Indicator | Unit | Method | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| Climate change (GWP100) | kg CO₂-eq | IPCC GWP100, mixed vintage across the aggregated defaults | characterized |
| Cumulative energy demand | MJ | total primary energy, fossil and non-fossil pooled | characterized |
| Water use | m³ | none — inventory-level consumption | inventory |
| Land occupation | m²·a | none — inventory-level occupation | inventory |
| Marine eutrophication | kg N-eq | ReCiPe 2016 midpoint (H) | characterized |
| Freshwater eutrophication | kg P-eq | ReCiPe 2016 midpoint (H) | characterized |
| Terrestrial acidification | kg SO₂-eq | SO₂-equivalent, CML / ReCiPe family | characterized |

Water and land are **inventory indicators, not impact indicators**: no AWARE or
equivalent water-scarcity characterization is applied, so a cubic metre in Sicily
and a cubic metre in Norway count the same; land is on-site occupation only, with
no transformation and no occupation embodied in purchased inputs — including none
for the agricultural land behind a heterotrophic sugar feedstock.

The receiving compartment is not modelled either: released nitrogen is counted
under marine eutrophication and released phosphorus under freshwater
eutrophication, wherever the discharge actually goes, where ReCiPe would
characterize by compartment.

Two numbers on `LCIAFactors` are **inventory assumptions, not characterization
factors**: `n_to_water_frac` (0.3) and `p_to_water_frac` (0.5), the share of
un-assimilated N and P assumed to reach water. The remainder is not tracked, so
neither element is closed to the environment.

A third sits on the scenario rather than on the background:
`system.substrate_carbon_fraction`, the carbon a kilogram of the organic feed
carries. It is declared per scenario (glucose by default) and reaches only the
carbon bookkeeping — see [CARBON_ACCOUNTING.md](CARBON_ACCOUNTING.md).

## 6. Coverage is not uniform

GWP and cumulative energy demand are the only categories most inputs reach. The
coverage matrix in the generated statement is the full picture; its shape is:

- **electricity** — every category except land;
- **heat, nitrogen, phosphorus, substrate** — GWP, CED, acidification, and one
  eutrophication category each for the two nutrients; no water, no land;
- **CO₂ supply, bicarbonate** — GWP (and CED for bicarbonate) only;
- **extraction solvent** — GWP and CED per preset, one global acidification
  factor, nothing else;
- **process water and site land** — carried straight from the inventory into
  their own category, with no factor in between;
- **declared materials and utilities** — whatever categories the scenario
  declares on the item itself.

A material or utility with no factor for a category contributes **nothing** to
it, which is not the same as contributing zero. Every such gap is reported:
`LCAResult.not_characterized` lists them per category for the scenario at hand,
`algametrix.lciamethod.completeness_report` renders them, and the generated
method statement tabulates them across the whole scenario suite.

To close a gap, declare the factor on the item:

```python
Material(name="Yeast extract", amount_per_kg=0.2, price=3.0,
         gwp=2.5, ced=30.0, acid=0.01, water=0.05, land=0.4,
         eutroph_n=0.002, eutroph_p=0.0005)
```

## 7. Allocation

ISO 14044 clause 4.3.4.2 asks that allocation be avoided by subdivision or
system expansion, then done on a physical relationship, and only then on another
relationship such as economic value.

| Step | What AlgaMetrix does |
|---|---|
| 1 — avoid | Single-product scenarios allocate nothing. A waste-derived feed may be handled by system expansion, and the credit is reported on its own line so a reader can remove it. |
| 2 — physical | Mass allocation between co-products, selectable per scenario. |
| 3 — economic | Revenue-share allocation. **This is the shipped default** for multi-product configurations. |

The default is step 3, not step 2, and that is a deliberate choice: mass
partitioning of a fractionated biomass assigns nearly the whole burden to a
low-value residual meal and nearly none to the pigment or lipid the plant exists
to make. ISO permits the choice and requires that it be declared and tested —
so both bases are selectable, and a study should report the result under each.
Re-running under mass and economic allocation is the sensitivity analysis clause
4.3.4.3 asks for.

Biogenic carbon is a separate declared convention with four modes; see
[CARBON_ACCOUNTING.md](CARBON_ACCOUNTING.md). Waste-derived feeds default to
cut-off; see [WASTE_FEEDS.md](WASTE_FEEDS.md).

## 8. What has been validated, and what has not

GWP and production cost have been compared against published studies (see
[VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md)). **Acidification, eutrophication, water and land
have not been compared against anything.** They are screening indicators built
on indicative factors, and they should be read as such.

## 9. Replacing the background

1. Edit `data/lcia.yaml`: the value, and with it the `source`, `geography`,
   `reference_period` and `quality` of every factor you touch.
2. Record the database name and version under `method.database`.
3. Re-run `python reproduce.py --only method` to regenerate the statement, and
   the test suite — `tests/test_lcia_method.py` refuses a factor that carries no
   provenance and checks that every declared factor still moves the category it
   is declared under.
