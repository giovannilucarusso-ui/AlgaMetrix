WHERE THE INPUTS OF THE STUDY RECONSTRUCTIONS COME FROM (SI S4.6)
==============================================================================
cost coefficients, inputs by origin, and the inputs each compared result depends on most
generated by reproduce.py on 2026-10-26

  An input is a numeric field of a reconstruction's scenario. A compared
  result depends on an input when a 1% change in the input moves the result.
  The elasticity d ln(result) / d ln(input) is estimated from that change; the
  input is raised, or lowered where a rise is not admissible, such as a
  recovery of 1. Near a recovery of 1 the lowered value multiplies the loss
  several times over, so a solvent recovery can carry a large elasticity where
  the solvent itself is a small cost. Inputs at zero have no elasticity and
  are not counted. Each input is put in one of four classes, against the
  publication its builder reconstructs:

  source      the source's own figure, or one that follows from its figures by
              a stated conversion.
  library     a default shipped with AlgaMetrix. A value from a library entry
              that was itself calibrated on the same source is counted as
              fitted.
  fitted      set so that the scenario reproduces a published result of the
              source, or taken from one, such as a published total operating
              cost. Part of the compared value is then on both sides of the
              comparison.
  assumed     set by the reconstruction with no location in the source
              recorded for it, including values taken from other publications
              or databases.

S4.6.1 COST COEFFICIENTS AND THE PLANT THEY REFER TO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Each reconstruction is run at the capacity of the plant its source costs,
  except the two SuperPro report cases: the algal-oil plant makes 3% more oil
  than the report's and the omega-3 plant has 11% more fermenter volume. The
  source and fitted cost coefficients are therefore used at, or close to, the
  size they were derived for, where the proportional cost model and a power
  law give about the same cost; 11% above the reference size, a six-tenths
  power law gives 4% less capital. Library coefficients were not derived from
  a costed plant of known size and have no reference capacity.
